  1. DefineYou + the Mind

    Start from your brief, held as a guess.

    The Mind interviews you to set out the brief and your thinking before the project starts. It reads your documents and scans web and social signals, which sharpen where to look but never count as evidence. The frame stays an assumption until customers say otherwise.

  2. Loop 1 · ProgressThe human part

    Start with progress, not products.

    Interviews about real past decisions: the last time someone switched, struggled or gave up, never “would you…?”. The Mind maps the four forces behind every switch, push and pull towards change, anxiety and habit holding people back, and the functional, emotional and social progress they wanted.

  3. Loop 2 · OutcomeThe Mind drafts · leadership agrees

    Pick one number to steer by.

    Leadership negotiates the business outcome. The team steers by a product outcome: a customer behaviour you can directly influence that leads the business number, with guardrails so you don’t win it by breaking something else. It stays a hypothesis that validated demand can challenge.

  4. Loop 3 · OpportunityAutomated · you approve

    Find the struggle worth solving.

    Struggling moments, workarounds and anxieties are organised into a tree by where they fall on the customer’s timeline, framed as problems, never features. Each has to pass admission tests. One target is chosen on demand against your ability to win; the rest are parked.

  5. Loop 4 · Causal LearningAutomated · reviewed with you

    Understand the system before you solve it.

    Mapped backwards from the outcome: what must be true for it to happen? The Mind separates what you control from the noise you don’t, and names the lead measure you can watch now and the lag measure that arrives later.

  6. Loop 5 · Delivery & ImpactThe Mind designs · your team ships

    Test the riskiest belief, cheaply.

    Several materially different solutions, each with its assumptions surfaced. The riskiest is tested with the smallest real-behaviour test, its bar written down before results. Killing a path before you build it is a win. Then you make the trade-offs, ship the smallest coherent increment, and read its impact at four levels.

  7. The Progress-to-Outcome TreeYou approve every change

    Every result updates the tree.

    Our evolution of the Opportunity Solution Tree: a supply spine and a demand spine that converge at the causal system. Every belief carries its evidence state, from assumption to emerging, validated or disproven, and validated demand can even challenge the outcome. Then you re-prioritise and go again.
