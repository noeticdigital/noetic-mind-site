An example: the same year, run again

This time, you learn first

Same team. Same idea. Same budget. But before anyone writes code, you find out what matters to customers. Noetic Mind guides you through it.

Day one · The Mind

You write down what you think

You think customers need a faster way to write reports. Noetic Mind records that as an assumption to test.

Weeks 1 and 2 · Listen

You sit down with customers

You ask about the last report they sent: what they were trying to get done, and what held them back. Writing it turns out to be easy. What takes hours is checking whether the numbers are right.

Week 3 · Aim

You pick one number

In this example it is revenue: where it stands today and where it needs to be. Every idea is judged against it.

Week 3 · Choose

You choose the problem worth solving

Customers described several struggles. Checking the numbers costs them the most, and solving it would move your number too. Now you have a completely different problem to solve.

Week 4 · Work back

You work back to something you can change

Start at the number and ask what would have to happen to move it. Keep asking until you reach something you can change now. Here, that is showing where each figure came from.

Weeks 5 and 6 · Test

You test before you commit

A few small versions go in front of customers, on real reports. You see which one helps. Then you decide what deserves to be built.

Month 12 · The question

This time, you have an answer

Someone asks why you built it. You show them what you assumed, what customers showed you, and what happened when you tried a fix. It all stays with your team, so the next decision starts from a better place.
