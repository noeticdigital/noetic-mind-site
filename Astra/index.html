The request

“Build a more powerful project dashboard.”

Raised by sales after two enterprise prospects asked for reporting.

The question underneath

Will a better dashboard help new account owners get their first client project ready?

What the evidence showed
  • EmergingNew account owners describe stalling before the first client invite, unsure what “ready” means.
  • EmergingOwners who invited a client early rarely mentioned reporting at all.
  • ChallengedOwners need more reporting power.
What changed our mind
  • ParkedDashboard expansion — revisit if activation moves and reporting requests persist.
  • Next testGuide five new account owners through first-project setup and watch where they stall.
Decision

Test setup guidance before committing to dashboard expansion. Committed

Why: the request described a symptom. The evidence points to setup uncertainty, which a dashboard won’t fix.

We already talk to customers. What changes?

The connection between those conversations and product decisions. The system makes clear which outcome you’re pursuing, which opportunity matters, what evidence supports it, and what assumption to test next.

Is this software or consulting?

It’s a managed operating system installed inside your team, with training and ongoing coaching. You’re not buying a standalone software subscription, and you’re not outsourcing discovery.

Who takes part, and how often?

A small cross-functional team — typically product, design, and engineering — runs one customer conversation and one decision review each week. Leadership joins once a quarter to agree the outcomes the team is working toward.
