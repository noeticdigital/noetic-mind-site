The difficulty

Most roadmaps rest on less evidence than they appear to.

  • Priorities follow whoever argued most forcefully, or most recently.
  • Work is delivered on time, and customers carry on exactly as before.
  • Research is commissioned once, written up, and out of date within the year.
  • When someone asks why this, and why now, the truthful answer is a hunch.

None of this is carelessness. Teams lack a dependable route from what customers need to what to build.

What Noetic Mind does

It starts with your market and your customers, and works back to what to build.

The Mind first builds a picture of your market and of what your team believes about it. Humans then interview your customers about decisions they have really made. The Mind sorts what they say into the needs behind it, sets those against the result your business has to achieve, and works back to the change most likely to deliver both. That change is tested on a small scale before anything is built, and the cycle begins again.

Each conclusion is shown with the evidence for it, so it can be examined, challenged and explained to anyone who asks.

At the centre · The Mind

First, the Mind learns your market and your team’s view of it

It reads what is said in public about your market: the web, reviews and social conversation. Your project lead answers a short brief, and each member of the team gives their own view of the product and the goal. From this the Mind writes a definition of what you are trying to understand. Every part of it is an assumption until the interviews test it.

Beside the definition it keeps a running summary of what the research has found. Both are kept current, from outside as the market moves and from inside as interviews and tests come in. The five steps that follow all feed it.

Step 1 of 5 · Listen

Begin with what customers actually did

With the definition in place, humans interview your customers about decisions they have really made: the last time they switched, struggled or gave up. A series of conversations is sorted by the job each customer was trying to get done.

Step 2 of 5 · Aim

Fix the result the business needs

Your team agrees an objective and one key result: a customer behaviour to change, where it stands today, and how far it has to move. Everything after this is judged against it.

Step 3 of 5 · Choose

Choose the customer need most worth meeting

The struggles customers described are set out in the order they met them. You choose the one that matters most to them and to your key result. The others are held for later, and stay in view.

Step 4 of 5 · Work back

Work back from the goal to what you can change

Starting from the key result, the route is worked out backwards, one step at a time, until it reaches something your team can change today. Then it is walked forwards to check that it holds.

Step 5 of 5 · Test

Test before building, then measure what changed

Several different solutions are tried on a small scale to see which best moves the key result agreed in step two. One passes and is built. Its effect is measured, and what was learned informs the next cycle.

What you have at any point

What a team comes away with

  • A stated degree of confidence

    Every belief is marked as an assumption, emerging, validated or disproven. Nobody has to guess how firm the ground is.

  • The next thing to find out

    The belief that matters most and is least supported, and the smallest test that would settle it.

  • Weak ideas dropped early

    Proposals are tested before they are built. Those that fail are set aside while they are still cheap.

  • Reasoning you can show

    The path from evidence to decision is written down, ready for a chief executive, a board or a sceptical colleague.

  • Knowledge that accumulates

    Each interview and each test adds to the same body of understanding. Nothing is filed away to date.

  • Shorter arguments

    Disagreements are referred to the evidence, which is quicker than referring them to seniority.

The steps

The Mind at the centre, and five steps around it

Most customer research ends with a report. It is useful for a while, and then it dates. Noetic Mind keeps the work going. It holds a definition of your market and a running summary of the research, both kept current. Each of the five steps below feeds it, and what is learned from testing informs the next cycle.

  2. AimAgree the one customer behaviour the business needs to change.

Principles

Principles

  • A feature request is evidence of a problem. It is seldom the solution.
  • A solution has to serve the customer and the business. One without the other is not worth building.
  • Desk research shows what is being said. Only interviews show why people act.
  • Confidence and evidence are different things.
  • An idea abandoned early has done its job.
  • The software proposes. People decide.
